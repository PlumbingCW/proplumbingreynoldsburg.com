Most faucet trouble is a slow drip. Sometimes it is not: a cracked supply valve under a sink can flood a cabinet in minutes. We dispatch 24/7 for faucet repair in Reynoldsburg, OH, and while you wait you should close the shutoff under the fixture or, if it will not turn, the main valve.

A faucet is a valve with a spout. Inside is a cartridge, ball, or pair of washers that meter water, and each wears out in its own way. Fixing the right part beats replacing the whole fixture every time.

Good to know

A drip is a countdown, not just a nuisance

A faucet that drips once a second wastes thousands of gallons a year, but the bigger problem is what it does to the seat. Water running past a damaged seat keeps eroding it, so a ten-minute cartridge swap this month can become a full fixture replacement next year.

Cartridges, aerators and local water

Columbus water averages about 7 grains per gallon of hardness, and Reynoldsburg buys it as a Columbus contract community. Hard-water minerals leave white crust on aerators and inside cartridges, which cuts flow and makes handles stiff. If your spout sputters or sprays sideways, unscrew the aerator and soak it in vinegar before assuming the faucet is bad.

When a supply valve breaks or a faucet sprays

A burst supply line or a snapped stop valve under a sink or toilet sends full house pressure into the room. Water damages cabinets, subfloor and drywall quickly, so this is an after-hours call. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule is open, and 24/7 dispatch covers the rest.

  • Turn the angle stop under the fixture clockwise to close it.
  • If that valve is stuck or broken, close the main shutoff.
  • Open a low faucet in the house to relieve pressure.
  • Move cleaning supplies and stored items out of the cabinet.
  • Do not force a seized valve, since it can snap off.

Burst and broken supply valves

The small chrome valves under sinks are quarter-turn or multi-turn stops, and many in older homes have not moved in decades. They seize with mineral deposits, and the stem can fail when you try to turn it. We replace a failed stop with a new quarter-turn valve and a braided line, so the next shutoff works when you need it.

If the main shutoff for the house will not close either, that becomes a larger repair, and we deal with it first because every other fix depends on it.

Tub and shower faucets, valves and drains

A tub spout that drips, or a showerhead that leaks when the water is off, usually means a worn cartridge or diverter behind the wall. Many tub valves can be serviced through the trim plate without opening tile, though a few older models need access from behind.

A shower or tub that fills with water around your ankles has a drain problem instead. Hair and soap form a plug just below the strainer, and a plastic drain snake often clears it. If the water returns in days, the line behind it needs a full cable or jet cleaning.

Bathtub faucet repair follows the same logic as the shower: shut the supply, remove the handle, pull the cartridge, and replace it.

Repair the faucet or replace it

A decision based on the fixture, not on habit.

Repair when

  • The finish is clean and the body is not corroded.
  • Parts are still sold for your brand.
  • The leak is at the cartridge, washer or seals.
  • The faucet is a quality brass body.

Replace when

  • The body is pitted or the finish is peeling.
  • Parts are obsolete or hard to match.
  • The faucet is wobbly on the deck.
  • You want a pull-down spray or a touchless feature.

Look under the sink first

These quick looks speed things up.

  • Look under the sink for wet cabinet walls.
  • Note whether the drip is at the spout or handle.
  • Check that the stop valve turns without force.
  • Take a photo of the brand name on the faucet.
  • Unscrew the aerator and look for crusted debris.

Safety steps before a plumber arrives

A failing faucet can still flood a cabinet or soak wiring nearby, so protect the room first.

  • Close the stop valve under the sink, turning it gently.
  • If it will not turn, shut off the main water valve.
  • Move cleaning products and cords out of the wet cabinet.
  • Do not force a corroded valve, since it can snap and spray.
  • Switch off the circuit if water reached an outlet or the disposal.
