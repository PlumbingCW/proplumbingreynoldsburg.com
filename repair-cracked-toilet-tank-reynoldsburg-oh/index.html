How to Repair a Cracked Toilet Tank in Reynoldsburg, OH

Hairline or split, a cracked tank needs a decision fast. Here is how to sort repairable from done.

By the Reynoldsburg Pro Plumbing teamOctober 16, 2026Reynoldsburg, OH

A cracked toilet tank in a Reynoldsburg, OH bathroom can be sealed only if the crack is a thin hairline above the waterline. Anything below the waterline, anything that runs through a bolt hole, or any crack that widens when the tank fills means the tank has to go. Porcelain does not flex, so a repair that holds water is a short-term patch, not a cure.

Below is how to tell which kind you have, what to do tonight, and when a plumber saves you a flooded floor.

How it works

Stop the leak first, diagnose second

Do these in order before you look at the crack.

1

Close the stop valve

Turn the oval handle on the supply line behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

2

Flush to empty

Flush once so the tank drains, then hold the handle to clear the last water.

3

Sponge the remainder

Dry the inside with a sponge and towel so you can see the full crack.

4

Dry the outside

Wipe the porcelain and floor, then check again after ten minutes for new beads of water.

5

Mark the crack ends

Use a pencil dot at each end. If the crack grows later, you will see it.

Quick guide

Which crack is which

Where the crack sits decides whether a repair is even worth attempting.

Hairline above waterlineCosmetic, no water contactEpoxy seal or leave alone

What an epoxy repair can and cannot do

A two-part waterproof epoxy putty made for porcelain can seal a thin crack if the surface is bone dry and clean. Rough the area lightly with fine sandpaper, press the putty into the crack, smooth it, and let it cure for the full time on the label before refilling. Plan on a day of no flushing.

Treat it as a bridge. Tank walls carry the weight of the water and the pull of the lid and bolts every day. A sealed crack that flexes can reopen, usually when nobody is home, which is how small bathroom leaks become ceiling stains in the room below.

Checklist

Tools and supplies for a patch attempt

Gather everything before you open the epoxy, because the working time is short.

  • Two-part waterproof epoxy putty rated for porcelain
  • Fine sandpaper and a clean rag with rubbing alcohol
  • Disposable gloves and a plastic putty knife
  • Sponge, towels, and a small bucket for leftover water
  • Adjustable wrench for the supply line nut
  • Painter's tape to hold a lid or part in place
  • Flashlight to inspect inside the tank walls
Side by side

Patch it yourself or call a plumber

The line is clearer than most people think.

Reasonable for a homeowner

  • Crack is a dry hairline above waterline
  • Tank is not leaking at the bolts
  • Supply stop valve closes fully
  • You are fine with a temporary fix

Better with a licensed plumber

  • Stop valve will not close or drips
  • Water is reaching the floor or ceiling below
  • Crack runs through a bolt hole
  • Tank wobbles on the bowl

When the crack is hiding a bigger leak

Not every puddle comes from the crack. A bad flapper, a worn wax ring, or a weeping supply line can leave the floor wet and send you hunting in the wrong place. Dry everything, tape tissue to the suspect spots, and watch which paper darkens first.

If the floor stays damp and you cannot find a source, the water may be traveling. Our leak detection visits trace hidden moisture, which is worth it when a subfloor or the ceiling below is involved.

Keeping the next tank from cracking

Most tank cracks start at the bolts. Rubber washers should seat snugly, and the nuts should be tightened a little at a time, alternating sides, until the tank sits level and stops rocking. Stop as soon as it feels firm, because porcelain breaks before metal complains.

Also keep the lid seated and lift it by the edges. A lid dropped on the rim is a common way to start a hairline that grows over a winter.

Sweating is not cracking

A tank that drips on the outside in summer may be sweating, not cracked. Cold water inside meets warm humid air, and moisture beads on the porcelain and runs down. The wetness is evenly spread, with no single spot where it starts.

Wipe the tank dry and watch it for an hour. If beads return across the whole surface, you have condensation, which a tank liner or a mixing valve addresses. If one thin line darkens first, you have found a crack, and the steps above apply.

Good to know

Replace, do not stack patches

If you have patched the same tank twice, stop. A third patch costs time and risks the floor. Replacing a tank or a full toilet is a standard job for toilet repair and installation, and a new tank seats on a fresh gasket so the old stress is gone. Local note

Reynoldsburg note: no permit for a toilet swap

Franklin County Public Health, which handles plumbing inspection for Reynoldsburg, says no permit is needed to replace a toilet (water closet). That removes one hurdle, so if your tank is beyond saving, swapping the toilet is not held up by paperwork.

Practically, that means you can compare a tank replacement against a whole new toilet on merit. A new set also lets you choose a taller or shorter tank that fits the space, and gives you fresh bolts, gaskets, and a flapper in one go. Confirm anything beyond the toilet itself, such as moving a drain, with FCPH or your plumber.
