A water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the main in the street to your house, and in Reynoldsburg, OH it often fails out of sight. Water line repair means finding the break in that pipe, then patching or replacing the damaged length.

If a leak is flooding your yard or your meter is spinning, close the main inside the house and call our 24/7 emergency dispatch. If the shutoff does not hold, keep the area clear and tell us on the phone.

The meter is still moving and the yard looks fine

Most homeowners first notice a buried line leak through the bill, a low-pressure shower or a patch of lawn that stays green and soft in a dry week. Plumbers call that a water service line leak. The pipe is cracked, pulled apart at a joint or corroded through somewhere between the meter and your foundation.

Close every faucet and appliance, then read the meter. If the dial still turns, water is escaping. If it stops when you close your main valve inside, the leak is on the house side of the valve. If it keeps turning with the main shut, the loss is outside, in the buried section.

Act first

When a buried line is flooding the yard

Water pouring across a lawn or into a window well from a broken service line is an emergency, and our 24/7 dispatch covers it. Same-day visits are possible for lines that are leaking but contained. Stop what you can from inside first.

  • Close the main valve inside the house
  • If the main will not close, find the curb stop and leave it to us
  • Keep children and pets off soft or sinking ground
  • Do not dig in the yard yourself before lines are marked
  • Turn off the water heater if the supply stays shut for long
  • Photograph the wet area and meter reading

Good to know

Who owns which part of the line

Part of the service line may belong to the utility and part to you. Columbus lists Reynoldsburg as a water and sewer contract community, so ask your water utility where its responsibility ends before you pay for a repair. Under OHIO811 guidance, privately owned lines are the homeowner's responsibility and may not be marked, so a private locator can be needed.

What to look for

Underground line leak signs

Buried leaks leave a few clues you can check from the yard and the basement.

Wet patch, no rain

Soft ground along the pipe path, especially between the meter and the house, points to a break close by.

Bill jumps with no cause

A rising bill when nothing in the house changed is the classic buried leak sign.

Sound of running water

A faint hiss near the entry point of the line, heard with every fixture off, is worth a call.

Muddy or discolored water

A break lets soil and sediment into the pipe when pressure drops, which can tint what comes out of the tap.

Damp basement wall

Water following the pipe into the foundation can show up inside as a wet patch on the wall near the entry.

Low water pressure from the line itself

A leak in the service line is one cause of weak pressure, but not the only one. A partly closed shutoff, a clogged pressure regulator or old galvanized pipe narrowed by scale can do the same. The pattern tells us which. Pressure that is low at every fixture and at every hour points to the line or the regulator. Pressure that is low at one tap points to that fixture.

We check pressure with a gauge at an outdoor spigot, watch how it drops when a fixture opens, and then decide where to dig or replace. If the pipe inside is the real choke point, a full repipe may make more sense than any work outside.

Water meter and yard box leaks

Where the line meets the meter, a leak at the connection or inside the box is a different case from a break in the pipe. The meter itself belongs to the utility, so we do not open or repair it. If we see wet soil around the box, we shut off on our side and point you to the right contact.

A damp lid on the meter pit after rain does not always mean a leak. Groundwater and runoff can pool there. Read the meter with everything off, then check again after an hour. That tells you which side to call.

New water lines and main replacement

Sometimes the answer is to install a fresh line rather than repair an old one, for example when a home addition, a detached garage or a new outdoor hookup needs supply. We lay the pipe, set the shutoff and pressure test before the trench closes. Questions on larger main replacement for a street or a property line go to the utility, and we help you ask the right questions.

If the work is part of a bigger project, see our plumbing installation work for how new runs and fixtures get planned together.

Checklist

Before you call about a suspected leak

These checks take ten minutes and sharpen the diagnosis.

  • Turn off every faucet, appliance and ice maker
  • Read the meter dial and wait ten minutes
  • Close your main valve and read again
  • Look for a wet or sunken spot along the yard
  • Note when the bill or pressure first changed
  • Mark the meter and shutoff locations for us
Cost factors

What changes the cost of a water line repair

A buried line has the widest cost range of any plumbing fix, because the ground decides how hard it is.

Depth and length

A short, shallow break near the foundation is quick. A long run across a yard takes more digging and more pipe.

Surface above the line

Lawn is easy to restore. Concrete, pavers and landscaping add demolition and rebuild time.

Pipe material

Old galvanized or lead-era fittings need more care. Modern pipe is lighter to handle and faster to join.

Method chosen

Open trench and trenchless approaches use different crews and equipment, with different tradeoffs.

Utility coordination

Locates and any work on the utility side can add waiting time before the dig starts.

Many homeowners policies treat buried line breaks differently from sudden indoor water damage, and some offer an optional add-on. Check your policy before assuming it is covered.

Digging in Franklin County and calling 811

Reynoldsburg sits in Franklin County, with small parts in Fairfield and Licking, so the office that handles inspection can depend on your address. Franklin County Public Health provides plumbing inspection across the county outside Columbus and Grove City, and its Reynoldsburg listing is on its plumbing page. Ask if your repair or replacement needs an inspection before the trench closes.

OHIO811 needs at least 2 working days' notice, not counting weekends, holidays or the day of the call, and no more than 16 calendar days ahead. A burst service line that is flooding your yard is an emergency, so ask us what can be done safely before marks are ready. Most nonurgent digs wait for the marks.
