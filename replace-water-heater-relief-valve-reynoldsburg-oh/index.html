Replacing a Water Heater Relief Valve in Reynoldsburg, OH

The relief valve is the last safety device on your tank. Learn how it fails, how it is swapped, and when you should not touch it.

By the Reynoldsburg Pro Plumbing teamOctober 16, 2026Reynoldsburg, OH

The relief valve on a water heater, called a T&P valve, opens when pressure or temperature inside the tank climbs too high. If it drips, sticks, or blows hot water, a Reynoldsburg, OH homeowner has two possible stories: the valve is worn, or the tank is overheating and the valve is doing its job. Replacing the valve fixes only the first. This guide shows how to tell the difference.

Good to know

Safety comes before any wrench work

A water heater stores a lot of hot water under pressure, and a failed safety valve can lead to scalds or tank rupture. Turn off the power at the breaker for an electric unit, or set a gas unit to pilot or off, then let the tank cool. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. Do not try this swap if you cannot shut off power or gas safely.

What to look for

Signs the valve needs attention

Not every drip means the part is bad. Match what you see to the likely cause.

Steady drip at the pipe

Mineral buildup on the seat or a worn spring. Often a valve problem.

Water spits in bursts

Pressure in the system is climbing. Check thermal expansion and water pressure.

Hissing or steam

The tank may be overheating. Shut off power or gas and call a plumber.

Corrosion at the outlet

Slow leaks leave crusty deposits. Replace the valve and the drain pipe if corroded.

Never opened, never tested

Valves can seize in place over time. A stuck valve will not open when it should.

Checklist

Parts and tools to have on hand

Match the valve to the tank label before you shop.

  • Replacement T&P valve with the same pressure and temperature rating
  • Pipe wrench and an adjustable wrench for the threaded fitting
  • Pipe thread sealant rated for potable water lines
  • A bucket and towels for the water in the tank
  • A discharge pipe of the right material that fits the valve outlet
How it works

How the swap goes for a competent DIYer

Only attempt this if the power or gas is off and the tank has cooled.

1

Cut power or gas

Turn off the breaker for an electric tank. For gas, set the control to pilot or off, and confirm no flame is lit.

2

Close the cold supply

Shut the cold inlet valve above the tank. Open a hot tap upstairs to relieve vacuum.

3

Drain a few gallons

Attach a hose to the drain valve and let the water level drop below the relief valve opening.

4

Remove the old valve

Disconnect the discharge pipe and unscrew the valve counterclockwise with the wrench.

5

Install the new valve

Wrap the threads with sealant, thread the valve in by hand, then snug with the wrench. Reconnect the discharge pipe, restore water, check for leaks, and then restore power or gas.

The discharge pipe is part of the safety system

The pipe that leaves the valve should point downward, end near the floor or drain, and never be capped or threaded at the end. A blocked pipe can prevent the valve from releasing. Keep the opening visible so you can see if it drips.

If your tank is in a closet or crawl space with no easy drain path, a plumber can reroute it safely. This is one of the reasons our water heater repair visits include a look at the discharge line and not just the valve.

When the tank, not the valve, is the problem

A new valve will not fix a tank that is overheating or leaking at the seams. If the valve keeps dripping within days of replacement, or the tank shows rust at the base, the heater is at the end of its service life.

At that point a swap costs more than it saves, and a plan for water heater replacement is the safer route. A plumber can match capacity and fuel type to the household and remove the old tank.

Local note

Reynoldsburg note: hard water and valve life

Columbus says it softens its water on average to 120 parts per million, or approximately 7 grains per gallon, and it lists Reynoldsburg as a water and sewer contract community. Even softened water leaves some mineral scale, and scale can keep a relief valve seat from closing. Lift the valve lever briefly once a year to flush it, with the discharge pipe pointing safely downward.

For permits, Franklin County Public Health inspects plumbing in Reynoldsburg. Ask FCPH or your plumber whether a permit applies to a water heater job, since this guide does not state a rule.

Source type: Columbus Division of Water hardness page, and Franklin County Public Health plumbing page.
