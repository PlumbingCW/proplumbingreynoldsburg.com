Garbage disposal repair in Reynoldsburg, OH means freeing a jammed flywheel, fixing a leaking seal or hose, or swapping a dead motor for a new unit. A disposal is a small grinder bolted under the sink drain, and it fails in predictable ways.

What to look for

What your disposal is telling you

The sound and the symptom point to the cause.

Hums, will not spin

Something is wedged between the impeller and the shredder ring. The motor is working, but it cannot turn.

Silent, no hum

Check the red reset button on the bottom. A tripped overload or a dead outlet is the usual cause.

Leaks from the bottom

A cracked housing or failed internal seal. This one usually means replacement.

Leaks near the top

A loose mounting ring, worn gasket or bad plumber's putty at the sink flange.

Smells even when clean

Food residue clings to the grinding chamber and the drain arm past it.

How it works

Freeing a jammed disposal safely

Most jams clear in a few minutes if you stay off the blades and the power.

1

Cut power

Turn the switch off, then unplug the unit or turn off its breaker. A switch alone is not enough.

2

Use the hex key

Many units have a hex socket in the center underneath. Work a hex key back and forth until the impeller moves freely.

3

Clear the chamber

Look in with a flashlight and pull out the obstruction with tongs. Never use your hand.

4

Press reset

Wait a few minutes for the motor to cool, then press the red button and run cold water.

When repair is not worth it

A jammed unit or a loose mounting ring is a cheap repair. A unit with a seized motor, a corroded housing or a bottom leak is not, because the part that fails is the part that holds everything together. Replacing the whole disposal usually costs less in labor than hunting a leak through a worn-out body.

Age matters. A disposal that has run for a decade and now trips the reset every few uses is near the end of its life. We will say that plainly, and let you choose.

Side by side

Batch feed versus continuous feed

If you are replacing, this choice affects how you use it.

Continuous feed

  • Wall switch turns it on and off.
  • Scraps go in while water runs.
  • More common and easier to find.
  • Lid-off operation needs care around fingers.

Batch feed

  • Runs only when the stopper is locked in.
  • Safer around children and pets.
  • Loads one handful at a time.
  • Slower, with fewer models to pick from.

What a disposal should and should not grind

Soft scraps and small bits of food go down fine. Grease, coffee grounds in volume, and fibrous items like celery, corn husks and onion skins do not, because they pack into the pipe. A disposal does not make fat disappear. It chops it into pieces that cool and coat the line, which is how a kitchen drain slows over months.

If your sink drains slowly even after the disposal runs clean, the problem is probably in the line. See our kitchen drain cleaning service for the next step.

Cost factors

What changes the price of disposal work

A reset and a replacement are far apart, so the price follows the work.

Repair or replace

Freeing a jam is brief. A new unit adds the cost of the disposal itself.

Horsepower

A bigger motor grinds more but costs more and needs more cabinet room.

Existing mounting

A reusable mount speeds the job. A corroded flange adds time.

Electrical and drain

A bad outlet or a clogged drain arm changes what the visit includes.

Insurance may respond to sudden water damage from a leaking disposal, but wear and tear on the unit itself is usually excluded. Ask your insurer how your policy reads.
