Good to know

Smell gas? Leave the building first

If you smell gas, rotten eggs or hear a hiss near the boiler, get everyone out and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call us. Do not flip lights, use a phone inside or try to relight a pilot. We will come after the gas company or the fire department says the building is safe.

Act first

When no heat is an emergency

A cold house is a safety issue when temperatures fall, especially with children, older adults or pets inside. Same-day and after-hours visits are how we handle boiler failures in winter, when available hours are the thing that matters. Here is what you can do safely while you wait.

  • Confirm the thermostat is on heat and calling for it
  • Check the boiler switch and the circuit breaker
  • Look at the pressure gauge, but do not add water unless you know how
  • Keep taps dripping if pipes are at risk of freezing
  • Open interior doors so warm air can spread
  • Never run a gas oven or grill to heat the house
What to look for

What a failing boiler tells you

Boilers give warning before they quit. These signs point to specific parts.

Banging or kettling

Scale or sludge on the heat exchanger overheats water in pockets and makes a rumbling, popping noise.

Pressure falls often

A leak in the loop, a bad expansion tank or a weeping relief valve lets pressure drop and the boiler lock out.

Radiators cold at the top

Trapped air stops hot water from filling the radiator, which a bleed valve may fix.

Boiler cycles on and off

Short cycling can come from an oversized unit, a bad thermostat or a clogged flue.

Pilot or ignition fails

A dirty flame sensor or a worn igniter stops the burner from proving flame and shuts it down.

Puddle under the unit

Water at the base can mean a leaking pump seal, a loose fitting or a cracked heat exchanger.

How it works

How we diagnose a boiler that will not heat

We work from the safest checks to the deepest.

1

Confirm safety

We check for gas and combustion issues and a clear vent before touching controls.

2

Read the controls

Thermostat call, aquastat, limit and error codes show whether the boiler is being told to run.

3

Check water side

Pressure, expansion tank and circulator pump determine whether heat moves through the loop.

4

Check fuel side

Igniter, flame sensor and gas valve show whether the burner can start and stay lit.

5

Repair and test

We replace the failed part, bleed the system if needed and run a full cycle.

Side by side

Repair or replace the boiler

A sound old boiler can run a long time, but some failures are the end of the line.

Repair is the sensible call when

  • The failed part is a pump, sensor, valve or igniter
  • The heat exchanger is sound and clean
  • Parts are available for the model
  • The boiler has not failed repeatedly

Replacement deserves a look when

  • The heat exchanger is cracked or leaking
  • Parts are no longer made for the unit
  • Repairs keep stacking up each winter
  • Efficiency is very low and fuel use is high

Pressure, air and the quiet failures

Most boiler calls are not a burned-out boiler. They are a lost-pressure problem, trapped air or a stuck circulator. A closed system should hold steady pressure, and a drop usually means water is escaping from somewhere or the expansion tank has lost its air cushion.

If you top off the system often, call before it becomes a habit. Fresh water brings dissolved air and minerals into the loop, which feed corrosion. The right fix is to find the leak, not to keep adding water.

Checklist

Before you call

A short check saves time and sometimes fixes the problem.

  • Confirm the thermostat is set above room temperature
  • Check the boiler switch and breaker
  • Note the pressure gauge reading
  • Write down any lit error code or blinking pattern
  • Find out whether the vent outside is clear of snow
  • Note any recent noise, leak or smell
Cost factors

What shapes the cost of boiler repair

Boiler work varies more than most repairs, because the boiler type and age change what is possible.

Part involved

A sensor or igniter is a small repair. A pump, gas valve or heat exchanger is a bigger one.

Age and model

Older units may need special-order parts and extra time to confirm compatibility.

System type

Steam, hot-water radiator and in-floor systems each have their own parts and their own troubleshooting.

Cause behind the failure

A repeat failure may need a deeper fix, such as cleaning the system or correcting a pressure issue.

After-hours visit

A winter-night callout is work done outside normal hours, and scheduling can affect the visit.

Boilers worn out from age are rarely covered, while sudden damage from a burst heating pipe may be. Check your policy and any home warranty you hold.

Licensing and boiler work in Ohio

Ohio licenses plumbing contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, and its plumbing and hydronics section covers heating water piping. You can check a license through Ohio's online license lookup before anyone works on your system. Ask for it, since hydronic work touches gas, water and venting.

Reynoldsburg sits in Franklin County, where Franklin County Public Health provides plumbing inspection outside Columbus and Grove City. Ask FCPH or your plumber whether a boiler or related piping replacement needs an inspection. For gas utility questions, ask your gas provider.
