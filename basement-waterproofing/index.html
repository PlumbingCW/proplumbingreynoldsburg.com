Basement waterproofing is any work that keeps groundwater and seepage from reaching your finished space. In Reynoldsburg, OH, we are plumbers, so our part is the drain lines, sump pits, pumps and leaking pipes that move water out.

Foundation repair and exterior sealing are a separate trade, and we do not do them. This page explains where each begins, so you can decide who to call first. Urgent plumbing leaks can still be called in 24/7.

What to look for

Damp walls, white streaks and a musty smell

Wet basements leave evidence. What you see tells you which trade is likely needed.

White powder on masonry

That chalky crust is mineral left behind as water moves through block or concrete and dries. It means moisture is passing through the wall.

Musty smell, no visible water

Damp air and wet insulation make that smell. The source may be seepage, a sweating pipe or a slow leak behind drywall.

Water after heavy rain

Water on the floor only during storms points to groundwater pressure, gutters, grading or a pump that cannot keep up.

Water at the floor edge

Seepage along the joint where wall meets floor often means the groundwater is pushing through the cove joint.

Wet spot with no rain

If it appears in dry weather, suspect a supply, drain or water heater leak, which is plumbing work.

How it works

How we sort out a wet basement

We rule out plumbing before anyone talks about foundations.

1

Check the pipes

We look for leaking supply, drain and heater lines, since a pipe leak is cheaper to fix than a wall.

2

Read the meter

A water meter that moves with everything off says the wetness is coming from our side, not the soil.

3

Look at the sump

If there is a pit, we check float, check valve and discharge. Many wet floors are a pump problem.

4

Map the water path

Where does it enter, when and how fast? That decides whether a drain or an outside fix is needed.

5

Refer the rest

If the cause is a wall or a grade problem, we say so and you can call a foundation or waterproofing contractor.

French drains and interior drain tile

A French drain is a perforated pipe in a gravel bed that collects groundwater and carries it to a pit or a discharge point. Inside a basement, it runs along the wall footing under the slab edge, and it ends in a sump. The plumbing portion is the pipe, the pit, the pump and the outlet.

The tradeoff is disruption. Installing an interior drain means cutting a trench in the slab, setting pipe and gravel, and patching the concrete. It lowers water pressure under the floor, but it does not seal a cracked wall. We can run the pipe and tie it into a working sump pump. Slab patching is concrete work, and we will say who to hire.

A plumbing leak that looks like a wet foundation

Homeowners sometimes spend big money on waterproofing and still have wet walls, because the source was a pinhole leak in a supply line inside the wall. A slow leak keeps one patch of the basement damp without any link to rain. It is why we check the pipes first.

If you suspect one, our leak detection work can trace moisture back to a pipe. Finding a failed fitting is far cheaper than excavating the outside of a house.

Checklist

What to check before you call

A few observations help us sort plumbing from structure.

  • Does water appear only after heavy rain or snowmelt
  • Is there a sump, and does it run
  • Where along the wall does the water show first
  • Are gutters and downspouts discharging away from the house
  • Does the water meter move with fixtures off
  • How high does the dampness climb on the wall
Cost factors

What drives the cost of plumbing-side waterproofing

The range is wide because every basement is different.

Length of drain line

A short run at one wall is a small job. A full perimeter drain is much bigger.

Slab and floor finish

Concrete cutting and patching adds time. A finished floor over the slab adds removal and replacement.

Sump and pump

A new pit, a new pump and a battery backup are separate items and raise the scope.

Discharge route

A pump that must send water far from the house needs longer pipe and a clear outlet.

Other trades needed

Foundation, masonry and exterior work are separate contractors, and their cost does not come through us.

Gradual seepage and groundwater entry are often excluded, while a sudden pipe break may be covered. Check your policy and any water backup endorsement before you start.
