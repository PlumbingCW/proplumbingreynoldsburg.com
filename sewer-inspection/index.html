A sewer inspection sends a waterproof camera through your main drain line so you can see cracks, roots, sags and blockages on screen, instead of guessing from symptoms. In Reynoldsburg, OH it is most often booked before a home purchase, after repeat clogs, or before lining a pipe.

If sewage is already coming up through a drain, call our 24/7 emergency dispatch. Otherwise a camera visit is a planned job, and we book it when it suits you.

Slow drains, sewer smell and odd gurgles, and what they point to

Gurgling after a flush, a slow floor drain, a damp yard patch, and a smell that comes and goes all share a few causes: a partial blockage, a crack that lets soil in, or a section of pipe that has settled and holds water. You cannot tell which from the symptoms alone.

The technical name for the fix to that guessing is a sewer camera inspection, sometimes called a video scope. It turns a vague symptom into a defect with a location, and that makes any later repair smaller and cheaper to plan.

What to look for

What the camera can show you

These are the findings we see most often on a recorded pass.

Root intrusion

Fine roots threading into a joint, or a thick mat blocking half the pipe.

Cracks and fractures

Longitudinal or circular splits where soil and water can enter.

Bellies and sags

Low sections that hold standing water and collect solids.

Offset joints

Pipe ends that no longer line up, catching paper and debris.

Scale and grease

Buildup that narrows the bore and explains slow flow.

Collapsed sections

Pipe that has crushed or caved, often visible as a hard stop.

How it works

What happens during a camera visit

Most inspections take well under a couple of hours, depending on access and line condition.

1

Find access

We use the cleanout, or pull a toilet if none is available, to feed the camera into the line.

2

Clear heavy debris

If the pipe is blocked, a light cleaning opens a path. The camera cannot see through sludge.

3

Record the run

The camera travels toward the street while a locator tracks its depth and position above ground.

4

Mark and measure

We note distances to each defect, and mark spots on the ground if you want repairs planned.

5

Review together

You watch footage, ask questions and get an honest read on which findings matter now and which can wait.

Inspections for Reynoldsburg home sales and older lines

Since Reynoldsburg is a Columbus sewer contract community, part of what runs under a street may be public and part private. Find out from the city where your private line ends, because footage of the public main is a different conversation than footage of your lateral.

If you are buying, ask the seller for cleanout access before the inspection and put the footage in your records. If a defect shows up, you can use it in negotiation and plan the fix with our sewer line repair service. We make no claims about local ordinance, and any rule on required inspections should be confirmed with your city.

Side by side

A camera pass versus cleaning and hoping

Two ways to deal with a line that keeps misbehaving.

Clean it again

  • Clears the current blockage quickly
  • Gives no view of pipe damage
  • May repeat the same visit weeks later
  • Leaves you unsure what is wrong

Camera first

  • Shows the actual cause of the clog
  • Records defects with distances
  • Lets you choose repair or maintenance
  • Makes later jetting safer for the pipe
Checklist

Before the camera arrives

A few minutes of preparation makes the visit smoother.

  • Locate your cleanout cap, often outside near the foundation
  • Clear stored items from the basement around the access point
  • Note which drains have been slow and for how long
  • Share any earlier cleaning or repair records you have
  • Ask the seller for access if you are the buyer
Cost factors

What affects the price of a camera inspection

The price comes before the work.

Access to the line

An exposed cleanout is quick, while a toilet pull takes longer.

Length of the run

A long run from a far corner of the house to the street takes more time.

Cleaning needed first

Heavy buildup has to be cleared so the camera can see.

Report or footage copy

Providing recorded video you can keep may add to the visit.

Insurance rarely pays for an inspection by itself. If a sudden failure caused damage, your insurer may ask for footage, so check your policy and keep the video.
