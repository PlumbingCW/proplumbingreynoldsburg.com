Water heater replacement is the removal of a worn-out or failed unit and the installation of a new one on the existing piping, vent, and power or gas supply. The aim is hot water again the same day.

How a tank fails, and why it cannot be patched

Inside a steel tank is a glass-like lining and a sacrificial anode rod that corrodes first to protect the steel. Once the rod is gone, rust works on the tank wall, and a pinhole opens that no clamp or sealant can hold. The water you see on the floor started as a weep that is now a stream.

That is why a leak from the shell is a replacement call, while a leak from a fitting or valve may not be. We open the panel, look at the base, and check the sediment before saying which situation you have.

Act first

A flooded heater and what to do first

If water is spreading across a finished floor, the clock matters more than the brand of tank. Call our 24/7 dispatch and describe where the water is, then work through the steps below so the crew finds a calm, dry workspace.

  • Close the cold valve on the supply line above the tank
  • Flip the water heater breaker off, or set gas to pilot off
  • Open one hot tap upstairs to stop the tank from siphoning
  • Smell gas? Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first
  • Sweep water toward a floor drain and lift anything stored on the floor
  • Photograph the tank label, since the model and size help us plan
How it works

What the replacement visit looks like

A straightforward swap follows this order, though every house adds its own surprises.

1

Shut and drain

Water, power, and gas go off. The old tank drains to a floor drain or hose line, which can take a while if sediment is thick.

2

Disconnect

Supply lines, vent, gas flex or wiring, and the relief valve line come apart. Corroded nipples sometimes need replacing here.

3

Set and level

The new tank goes on a pan or stand as needed, with the controls facing out where they can be reached later.

4

Reconnect and vent

New flexible connectors go on, the vent is checked for slope and clearances, and a thermal expansion tank is added where the system calls for one.

5

Fill, fire, test

We purge air, check every joint for weeping, light or energize the unit, and confirm temperature at a faucet.

Tank, tankless, or heat pump: what the choice changes

Most homes simply replace like for like, but the alternatives affect the house in different ways.

TypeWhat it asks of the houseMain tradeoff
Standard tank, gasExisting vent and gas line, usuallyLower upfront effort, ongoing standby heat loss
Standard tank, electricA dedicated circuit and room for the tankSimple, but slower recovery under heavy use
TanklessLarger gas line or high amperage, new ventEndless supply, more installation work and scale care

Good to know

Switching fuel is a building project, not a swap

Moving from gas to a heat pump heater means a new 240 volt circuit, a condensate path, and space for air to move. It can be a good long-term move. It is also an electrician and plumber job, so ask for it by name instead of expecting it to happen on a standard replacement visit.

No hot water at all, and what it may mean

A heater that has gone completely cold is not always a dead tank. Dead elements, tripped limits, and failed pilot sensors all look the same from the shower. If the unit is more than a decade old and the base is wet or rusty, we recommend replacement. If it is dry inside and the failure is a part, our repair service is the cheaper road.

Reynoldsburg, OH

Columbus water, tank life, and the permit question

Columbus softens water to about 120 ppm, or roughly 7 grains per gallon, and Reynoldsburg is on its list of water contract communities. Even softened water leaves scale on the bottom of a tank and wears an anode over the years. Flush the new tank each year and ask us to check the anode before the warranty paperwork runs out.

Franklin County Public Health inspects plumbing in Reynoldsburg and in Franklin County outside Columbus and Grove City. Whether a permit applies to a water heater swap is something to ask Franklin County Public Health plumbing office or to put to your plumber before work begins, so nothing is left open on a sale or insurance review.

Side by side

Replace now or limp along

An old tank can seem fine until it is not. These are the factors that tip the decision.

Reasons to replace soon

  • Rust streaks or moisture at the base of the tank
  • Rumbling, popping, and recovery that keeps slowing
  • Tank has gone past a decade of service
  • Repairs have started stacking up in a single year

Reasons to hold off

  • Leak is at a fitting, not the shell
  • Tank is young and sediment is light
  • Only an element or thermostat has failed
  • You are planning a fuel change and need to line up parts

Cost factors

What moves the price of a replacement

A replacement quote is built from the unit and from what the house requires around it.

Unit type and size

A larger tank, a high-efficiency model, or a hybrid each cost more than a standard tank of the same capacity.

Vent condition

Old galvanized or rusted flues often have to be replaced when a new burner goes in.

Code items nearby

Expansion tanks, new shutoff valves, a pan, and a relief line may be added to bring the setup up to current practice.

Access and removal

Stairs, a crawlspace, and old tank hauling all change labor time.

After-hours work

Night calls follow a different schedule than a same-day booking.

If water from the old tank damaged floors or drywall, a policy may cover the sudden damage but not the heater itself. Your insurer decides, so ask before throwing anything away.
