Signs You Need Your Drains Cleaned in Reynoldsburg, OH

Slow sinks and gurgling pipes rarely fix themselves. Here is how to read the warning signs in your Reynoldsburg, OH home.

By the Reynoldsburg Pro Plumbing teamOctober 16, 2026Reynoldsburg, OH

A drain needs cleaning when water leaves slower than it should, makes noise on the way out, or smells like something is sitting in the pipe. In Reynoldsburg, OH homes, the usual cause is a slow buildup of grease, soap film, hair, and wipes that narrows the pipe until one more bad week closes it.

Catching the signs early keeps a ten minute chore from turning into a backed-up floor drain. This guide covers what each symptom means, which ones you can test yourself, and the point where a plumber with a drain machine is the better choice.

What a clog really is

Most people picture a clog as a plug, like a wad of hair stuck in one spot. Real clogs grow more like plaque in an artery. A thin coat of kitchen grease catches soap scum, the scum catches hair and food bits, and the opening shrinks a little each week.

That is why a drain can seem fine on Monday and dead by Saturday. The pipe was already mostly closed, and one heavy load of laundry or a large pot of pasta water was all it took to finish the job.

What to look for

Six warning signs to watch for

Any one of these can be a fluke. Two or more at once point to a buildup you should deal with soon.

Water drains slowly

A sink or tub that takes a full minute to empty has a narrowed pipe. It rarely gets better on its own.

Gurgling after flushing

Air is fighting past a partial blockage. Listen for bubbling in a tub or shower when the toilet runs.

Smells that keep returning

A sour or swampy odor that comes back after you rinse the drain means residue is rotting inside the pipe.

More than one fixture affected

If the shower and the bathroom sink both run slow, the problem is likely in the branch line they share.

Standing water in the basement

A wet floor drain or a basement tub that fills when you do laundry is a main line warning.

Fruit flies near drains

Tiny flies hovering at a sink point to organic film. They breed in it, so the film is thick enough to feed them.

How it works

What to try before you call

These are safe, low-risk steps. Stop if water starts rising instead of falling.

1

Remove the stopper

Pull out the pop-up or strainer and wipe off the hair mat. In bathroom sinks and tubs, this fixes a surprising share of slow drains.

2

Flush with hot water

Run the hottest tap for two minutes. This helps with fresh grease but does little for hair or soap scale.

3

Clean the trap

Set a bucket under the U-bend, unscrew the slip nuts by hand, and empty the trap. Check the washers before you reassemble.

4

Use a hand auger

Feed a small drum auger in slowly, crank clockwise, and pull the snag out. Do not force it past a hard stop.

5

Skip the harsh chemicals

Caustic drain openers can sit in a fully blocked pipe and burn your skin or damage old fittings. They also make the next plumber's work riskier.

Side by side

Home cleaning versus professional cleaning

Both have a place. The question is how deep the buildup is and how far away it sits.

Reasonable to do yourself

  • Hair clogs in a tub or bathroom sink
  • A trap you can reach under one sink
  • Light kitchen grease in a short run
  • Pop-up stoppers and strainers

Better left to a plumber

  • Backups in more than one fixture
  • Water in a basement floor drain
  • Odors that return within days
  • Any clog that cabling did not clear
Checklist

Habits that slow the buildup

Small changes at the sink keep the pipe open between cleanings.

  • Scrape plates into the trash instead of the kitchen sink
  • Pour cooled cooking fat into a can, not down the drain
  • Fit a mesh catcher over every shower and tub drain
  • Flush only toilet paper, even if wipes say flushable
  • Run hot water for a full minute after washing greasy dishes
  • Clean pop-up stoppers on the first of every month

How professionals clear a line

A plumber starts by learning how far the blockage sits. A hand cable works for short runs. For a main line, a motorized drain machine with a cutting head scrapes the pipe wall instead of poking one hole through the middle.

For heavy grease or roots, high pressure water can scour the inside of the pipe. If the same line keeps clogging, a camera shows if you have a belly, a crack, or a root intrusion. Our drain cleaning service starts with the symptom and works toward the least invasive fix, and larger main lines get the full sewer line cleaning treatment with a machine sized to the pipe.

When a slow drain becomes an emergency

Stop using water and call if sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, if several fixtures back up together, or if you smell sewer gas in the house. Do not run the washer or dishwasher.

Local note

Reynoldsburg note: who owns which part of the sewer

Columbus lists Reynoldsburg, Gahanna and Whitehall among its Water and Sewer Contract Communities. That tells you the city is tied into a larger regional system, but it does not say which pipe belongs to you and which to the utility. Before you dig or replace anything, ask your utility where private responsibility starts.

For drain cleaning, the practical point is simple. A clog that appears only in your house plumbing is yours to fix. A backup affecting the street or neighbors may be a main line problem, so mention it when you call, and note the time of day it happens.
