Cleaning Smelly Drains in Reynoldsburg, OH Homes

A bad smell is a clue. Match the odor to the cause before you pour anything down the drain.

By the Reynoldsburg Pro Plumbing teamOctober 16, 2026Reynoldsburg, OH

Smelly drains in a Reynoldsburg, OH house come from three places: a dry or dirty trap, a buildup of grease and hair along the pipe wall, or a vent or sewer problem further down the line. Cleaning the drain fixes the first two. The third needs a plumber to find the source.

Start with where the smell lives. One drain smelling means a local cause. Every drain smelling means the main line, so the steps below start there.

Quick guide

Read the smell before you pick a fix

Different odors point to different problems.

SmellLikely causeFirst move
Rotten eggSewer gas through a dry trap, or bacterial filmRun water, then clean the trap
Musty or sourBiofilm and hair inside a tub or sink drainHot flush and enzyme cleaner
Raw sewageMain line partly blocked or a vent problemStop and call a plumber
ChemicalOld drain cleaner residue or a nearby leakFlush well, check for leaks
Rotting foodDisposal or kitchen drain buildupClean the disposal and trap
Gas-like in the whole houseCould be natural gasLeave and call the gas utility or 911
Good to know

If you smell gas, do not stay to investigate

Sewer gas and natural gas can smell alike. If the odor is strong, fills a room, or comes with dizziness, get everyone outside and call the gas utility or 911 from there. Do not flip switches or light anything. Drain troubleshooting can wait until a professional says the air is safe.

How it works

A step-by-step drain refresh that costs almost nothing

Work in this order on one drain at a time.

1

Run the water

Pour a quart of water into every drain you rarely use, especially floor drains and guest bath sinks, to refill a dry trap.

2

Clear the surface

Pull the stopper or strainer, remove hair and slime, and wipe it with a disposable towel.

3

Hot flush

Flush with very hot tap water for a minute. Avoid boiling water on PVC fittings or cracked porcelain.

4

Baking soda and vinegar

Use half a cup of each, wait fifteen minutes, then rinse. This loosens light film but does not clear a clog.

5

Open the trap

Place a bucket, unscrew the slip nuts of the P-trap, and clean it by hand. Reassemble hand tight.

Why the trap matters more than the cleaner

Every sink, tub, and floor drain holds a small bend of water called a trap. That water is a seal, and it keeps sewer gas out of the room. When a trap dries from lack of use, gas comes straight up.

Rarely used basement and utility drains are the usual culprits, along with the tub in a spare bathroom that nobody showers in. Homes that sit empty for a few weeks can smell sharply on the day you return. A cup of water poured monthly keeps the seal in place. A few drops of mineral oil on top slow evaporation, though a cleaner plumbing habit is simply to run the water.

The kitchen and the disposal are their own case

Kitchen drains smell because fat cools and sticks. A garbage disposal adds food scraps trapped under the splash guard. Scrub the underside of the rubber flange with a brush and dish soap, then grind a handful of ice and a halved lemon to knock off residue.

Keep your hand out of the disposal and switch the power off at the wall before you reach near it. Pouring grease down the sink is the habit that brings the smell back, so wipe pans with a paper towel first.

Checklist

Stop DIY and call a plumber when

These signs mean the cause is beyond a trap or a strainer.

  • Every drain in the house smells at the same time.
  • Water backs up in a tub when the washer drains.
  • Gurgling noises come from toilets and sinks together.
  • The smell returns within a day after a thorough cleaning.
  • You see wet floor or sludge around a floor drain.
  • The kitchen drain stays slow after you cleaned the trap.
Side by side

Snaking, cleaning, or jetting

Which tool fits depends on how deep the buildup sits.

Cable cleaning fits when

  • A clog sits close to the fixture
  • The drain is slow but not fully blocked
  • Hair or soft debris is the cause
  • The pipe wall is mostly clean

Hydro jetting fits when

  • Grease and film coat the whole pipe
  • Cable cleaning gave only short relief
  • Smells return after each cleaning
  • A camera shows heavy buildup

What a plumber does that a bottle cannot

A plumber starts by finding how far the smell travels. We check each fixture, listen for gurgles, and run a camera when the pattern suggests the main line. That tells us whether the film is on the wall or whether a cracked pipe is leaking gas.

Routine buildup is handled by professional drain cleaning, and heavy grease or scale calls for high-pressure jetting that scours the whole wall. Local note

Reynoldsburg note: who owns the sewer problem

Columbus lists Reynoldsburg, Gahanna, and Whitehall among its Water and Sewer Contract Communities. That is a billing and service relationship, and we cannot say which part of any single street's sewer belongs to which agency.

The practical step is simple. If the smell is in every drain and a plumber's camera shows the problem outside your property line, call your water and sewer utility with the findings. If the trouble is inside, it is yours to fix, so get a clear price first.
