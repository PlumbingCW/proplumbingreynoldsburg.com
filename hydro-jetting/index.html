Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that pushes water through a hose and a nozzle at very high pressure, so the spray strips grease, sludge and root hairs off the pipe wall. If your line in Reynoldsburg, OH keeps backing up after being snaked, jetting is usually the next conversation.

Backups do not keep office hours, and our round-the-clock emergency dispatch covers them. While you wait, stop running water, keep toilets unflushed, and pull rugs back from the lowest drain in the house.

Snaked it and it clogged again? Here is why

A drain cable is a spinning corkscrew. It bores a hole through the blockage, water drains, and everybody feels good for a few weeks. But the cable leaves a ring of grease and scale clinging to the pipe wall, and that ring is the seed for the next clog.

Roots behave the same way. A cable chops the part of the root mass that crosses the pipe, yet the fine hairs and the cut stubs stay in the joint and regrow. If you have paid for two or three snaking visits on the same line, you are not dealing with bad luck. You are dealing with buildup that a cable cannot remove, and that is the situation jetting was built for.

What to look for

What a jetter clears that a cable leaves behind

Water does the work, so the nozzle reaches places a blade cannot. These are the usual targets.

Kitchen grease coating

Cooled fat sticks to the pipe wall in a waxy layer. Hot, high-pressure water cuts it loose and flushes it downstream.

Fine root hairs

A jet nozzle with forward and rear ports shaves the hair-like roots in joints, where a cable head only pokes through.

Mineral scale

Scale narrows older lines until a normal flush overwhelms them. Jetting scours the wall back toward its full diameter.

Soap and hair sludge

Bathroom branches collect a gray paste of soap and hair. The spray breaks it up instead of drilling one channel through it.

Sand and debris

Silt that entered through a cracked joint settles in low spots. The jet pushes it out to where it can be removed.

Side by side

Cable versus jet on the same clog

Neither tool is always right. Here is how we weigh them at your cleanout.

Cable (snake)

  • Quick and light for a fresh single blockage
  • Opens a channel but leaves wall buildup in place
  • Good for small branch lines and sink traps
  • Can tear through a weak or offset pipe joint

Hydro jet

  • Cleans the full circle of the pipe wall
  • Handles heavy grease, scale and root hairs
  • Needs a sound pipe and a cleanout to enter
  • Rinses debris out instead of pushing it ahead

How it works

How a jetting visit runs at your house

The order matters, because jetting a pipe you have not looked at is guessing.

1

Find the cleanout

We locate the access point, usually a capped pipe low on an outside wall or by the basement floor. No access means we discuss how to add one.

2

Camera look

A small camera runs through the line first, so we see the real blockage and any cracked, bellied or offset pipe before water goes in.

3

Choose the nozzle

Grease calls for a different spray pattern than roots or scale. The nozzle and pressure follow what the camera showed.

4

Jet the line

We work from the clog back toward the cleanout, so loosened debris travels downstream and out instead of packing behind us.

5

Verify the result

A second camera pass confirms the wall is clean and shows what is left, such as a root-entry joint that needs repair.

Act first

When a backing-up main line needs jetting right away

Sewage coming up through a floor drain or the lowest tub is a health problem and it grows by the hour. Call us for 24/7 emergency dispatch, and we will tell you if jetting fits or if a cable has to open the line first.

  • Stop using sinks, showers, washers and dishwashers immediately
  • Do not flush, even if the toilet seems to drain
  • Move belongings away from the basement floor drain
  • Keep children and pets out of any standing wastewater
  • Do not pour chemical drain opener into a backed-up line

Roots, grease and the camera that sorts them out

We pair jetting with a camera because the same symptom has different causes. A line packed with grease needs cleaning and a change in what goes down the sink. A line with roots needs cleaning now and, usually, a repair plan later.

Jetting clears roots for a while but does not stop them. If the camera shows roots entering at a joint, expect them back, and the honest options are scheduled cleaning or fixing that joint. We lay that out before any repair work, and our camera inspection visits can be booked on their own when you want the picture first.

Checklist

When we will not jet a line

Water pressure is a tool, and it has limits. We skip it in these cases.

  • Pipe is collapsed or badly offset, so a jet could not pass
  • Old clay or thin cast iron already crumbling at the joints
  • No cleanout exists and the roof vent is the only access
  • Blockage is a solid object that must be retrieved by hand
  • Fixtures upstairs would flood if water bounced back

Private line, public main, and what that means in Reynoldsburg

Because Reynoldsburg buys Columbus sewer service as a contract community, a backup can sit on your private line or on the public main. Jetting applies to your private side. If the camera shows trouble past the property line, the utility or your city has to answer for that stretch.

If jetting turns into a dig, the homeowner side of the work needs a call to OHIO811 first, and the contractor doing the work places that call. Utilities flag only what they own, so a private locator may be needed for your own pipe. Count at least 2 working days, so plan repairs around it, and read our sewer line repair options before you decide.

Good to know

A cleaner pipe is not a repaired pipe

Jetting removes what is in the pipe. It does not close a crack, level a bellied section or seal a joint. If the camera finds those, a clean line only buys time, and we will say that plainly so you can decide whether to schedule the repair.

Cost factors

What shapes the price of a jetting visit

You get the price stated before we start. These items move it.

Line length and size

A long run of 4 inch pipe takes more hose and time than a short 3 inch branch.

How heavy the buildup is

Thick grease or a root mass can take several passes, while light scale may take one.

Access to the line

A clear cleanout is quick. Adding one, or pulling a toilet to reach the line, adds labor.

Camera work

A before and after camera pass costs time, but it prevents jetting a damaged line blind.

Hour of the call

A midnight call and a daytime visit are priced differently.

Insurance seldom pays for cleaning out a clog caused by gradual buildup. Sudden damage such as a sewage backup may be covered under some policies, so check your policy and ask your insurer before work starts.
