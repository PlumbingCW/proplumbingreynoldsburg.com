Plumbing installation means putting new water, drain, vent or gas piping and fixtures into a building, whether a single sink or a full house. Our licensed plumbers install for homeowners and builders in Reynoldsburg, OH and the rest of Franklin County.

Good installation work is mostly planning: where the pipes go, how the drains slope, where the vents leave the roof, and who inspects what before a wall closes. Urgent problems can still be called in at any hour.

Good to know

Smell gas? Leave first, call us second

If you smell gas, hear a hiss near a gas line or appliance, or feel sick with no clear cause, leave the building right away. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside, or light anything. From outside, call your gas utility or 911 before you call us.

We can inspect and install gas piping once the utility has made the situation safe. We do not try to chase an active gas leak with the occupants inside.

What installation covers

Fixtures are the visible part: sinks, toilets, tubs, showers, laundry hookups, outdoor spigots, water heaters and disposals. Behind them is the pipe, in three systems. Supply brings water in under pressure, drain carries waste out by gravity, and vent lets air in behind the water so drains empty without gurgling.

Each system has its own rules about size, slope and fitting types, which is why a handyman swap and a plumber's rough-in are not the same job. For a planned tank swap, see how water heater installation works.

How it works

From rough-in to final connection

New work happens in two visits with an inspection in between.

1

Plan the layout

We mark fixture spots, check joist and stud space, and choose pipe sizes.

2

Rough-in

Supply, drain and vent pipes are set in the open framing before walls are closed.

3

Test and inspect

Pipes are pressure or water tested, and the work is left open for the inspector where one applies.

4

Set fixtures

After walls and floors are finished, we set the toilet, sinks, tub and faucets.

5

Final test

We run every fixture, check traps and shutoffs, and look for slow drains.

Gas line inspection and installation

Gas piping runs to furnaces, boilers, water heaters, ranges and dryers, and each appliance needs the right pipe size for its load. We inspect existing lines for corrosion, loose fittings and missing shutoffs, then pressure test any new piping before it is put into service. That test is what proves a joint will not leak.

A dedicated gas page is planned for this site. Until then, treat any gas work as a licensed-trade job and not a weekend project, because a small leak is a fire and carbon monoxide risk.

Side by side

What you can do yourself and what to hand off

Some jobs reward a handy owner. Others do not.

Reasonable to do yourself

  • Swap a faucet or a showerhead.
  • Replace a toilet fill valve or flapper.
  • Install a new supply line to a sink.
  • Add an aerator to a faucet.

Better handed to a plumber

  • Anything with gas piping or a gas valve.
  • New drain and vent runs.
  • Moving a toilet or a tub.
  • Tying into the main water line.

Inspection and permits in Franklin County

Franklin County Public Health provides plumbing inspection for Reynoldsburg, and it says no permit is needed to replace a faucet, sink or toilet. For anything beyond a straight swap, such as new drain lines or a new bathroom, ask FCPH whether an inspection applies and how to request it. Have your address ready, since parts of Reynoldsburg sit in Fairfield and Licking counties.

Ohio licenses plumbing contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, and you can check a license through Ohio's online license lookup. If you are digging for a new yard line, the contractor must notify OHIO811 first and utilities mark their lines. Privately owned lines are your responsibility.

What to look for

New construction plumbing basics

Builders and owners planning a new house should settle these early.

Fixture count

Every bath, kitchen and laundry adds supply and drain load, so size the lines for the final plan.

Water heater location

Central placement shortens hot water wait and keeps vent routes simple.

Drain slope

Gravity drains need continuous fall, which limits where basement bathrooms can go.

Shutoff access

Isolation valves at each bath save a lot of trouble later.

Test before drywall

A pressure test while walls are open is the cheapest leak check you will ever do.

Cost factors

What an installation price depends on

Installation jobs vary more than repair jobs, so the price follows the scope.

Rough-in or finish only

Running new pipe through framing is more work than setting a fixture on existing lines.

Pipe and fixture choices

Material and fixture grade change both parts and labor.

Access and demolition

Finished walls and tight spaces add time.

Inspections

Where an inspection applies, the schedule includes waiting for it.

Gas or special lines

Gas piping, sump discharge or a pump adds equipment and testing.

Planned installations are normally not covered by insurance. If a failed fitting later causes sudden water damage, coverage depends on your policy and the cause, so check with your insurer.
