Sewer Pipe Line Repair for Reynoldsburg, OH Homes

Find the break with a camera first. Then choose between spot repair, lining, or replacement.

By the Reynoldsburg Pro Plumbing teamOctober 16, 2026Reynoldsburg, OH

Sewer pipe line repair in Reynoldsburg, OH starts with finding out what is wrong, not with digging. The lateral that runs from your house to the main can crack, shift at a joint, sag into a belly, or fill with tree roots. Each of those has a different fix, and a camera inspection tells you which one you have.

This guide covers the signs, the repair options and their tradeoffs, and the legal step that comes before any shovel goes in the ground.

What to look for

Signs the sewer line is failing

One sign can be a simple clog. Two or three together point at the line itself.

Slow drains everywhere

Every fixture is sluggish, not just one sink or tub.

Gurgling toilets

Air is pushed back through traps when flow is restricted.

Sewage smell in the yard

A cracked pipe can leak gas and waste into the soil.

Soggy or sunken lawn

A wet patch or dip over the pipe route may mark a break.

Repeat clogs

Cleaning helps for a few weeks, then the problem returns.

Basement backup

Waste rises in the lowest drain when the line is fully blocked.

The right option depends on the damage, the depth, and what sits above the pipe.

Why sewer lines fail in the first place

Older lateral materials such as clay and cast iron break down at joints, which lets roots in. Newer plastic pipe can still sag if the trench settles or if a heavy load sits over it. Ground movement, a nearby tree, or years of grease can each play a part.

That is why a repair quote without a camera recording deserves a second look. The pipe cannot tell you what failed, but the footage can, and it also shows whether the rest of the run is sound enough to leave alone.

Side by side

What you can do yourself and what to leave

There is a short list of safe steps. Everything involving digging or raw sewage is not on it.

Safe for the homeowner

  • Locate the cleanout cap and keep it clear
  • Stop flushing wipes and pouring grease
  • Cut back tree roots near the yard line
  • Note the dates and fixtures of each backup

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Opening a cleanout during a backup
  • Any excavation near the sewer line
  • Cutting or lining a pipe
  • Handling waste in a flooded basement

If waste is coming up right now

Stop running water in the house, since every drain feeds the same line. Keep children and pets out of the area, and avoid touching the contaminated water. Open windows if the smell is strong, and do not use electrical devices in a flooded space.

Preventing the next failure

After a repair, the habits matter. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down, and grease hardens in the lateral and catches everything behind it. Keep a clear cap on the cleanout, and plant new trees well away from the pipe route.

A camera look every few years, especially on clay or cast iron, on an older line is cheap compared with a surprise backup. It also gives you a record of the pipe's condition when you sell the house or file an insurance question.

Good to know

Cost comes from depth, length, and what is above

The price of a sewer repair moves with how deep the pipe sits, how long the damaged section is, what covers it, and how easy it is to reach. A line under a lawn is a different job from one under a driveway or patio. Real numbers come only after the camera shows the break, so be wary of fixed prices given over the phone.

Local note

Reynoldsburg note: call OHIO811 before any dig

Ohio law requires whoever digs to notify OHIO811 first, by dialing 811. The notice must be given at least 2 working days ahead, not counting the call day, weekends, or legal holidays, and no more than 16 calendar days ahead. The contractor doing the work makes that notification, so ask yours to confirm it.

Utilities mark the lines they own. Privately owned lines, including some service lines, may not be marked, and OHIO811 says they are the responsibility of the homeowner, so a private locator may be needed. For a sewer repair, that means a lateral can sit unmarked, and the camera locator is how we find it.

Source type: OHIO811 homeowner page (oups.org).
