Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes inside your house, the lines that carry water to every tap, tub and appliance. Homeowners in Reynoldsburg, OH usually start asking about it after a second or third leak in the same year.

It is a big decision, so we spend the first visit working out if you need a full job, a partial one, or just a repair. If a pipe is spraying water right now, shut the main valve and call us any hour, since we dispatch 24/7.

Weak pressure, brown water and leaks that return

These three symptoms are what most people describe before they hear the word repipe. Pressure that falls when two fixtures run, a rusty tint on the first draw of the morning, and a pinhole that reappears a few feet from the last patch all point at pipe walls that are failing from the inside.

The technical cause is corrosion and scale. Steel pipe narrows as rust and minerals build up inside it, and once the wall is thin in one place it is thin in many. That is why chasing one leak at a time rarely ends the problem.

What to look for

Signs the pipes themselves are the problem

A single leak is repair territory. A pattern is not.

Pressure drops at the tap

The main valve is fully open and the meter is fine, but the shower dribbles. Mineral buildup narrows older pipe.

Discolored first draw

Orange or brown water after sitting overnight means iron pipe is shedding rust.

Pinhole leaks nearby

A pinhole fix last year and another this winter on the same run. The rest of the pipe is similar.

Green crust on copper

Blue-green stains at fittings show copper corroding or fittings leaking slowly.

Water bill creeping up

No fixture is dripping, yet use rises. A hidden weep may be feeding a wall cavity.

Pipe materials and the tradeoffs

No material wins on every count. This is how the common ones compare.

CPVCRigid plastic, low cost to installBrittle with age, tends to crack at stress points

Side by side

Full repipe or partial repair

The right scope depends on how much pipe is sound.

Partial work fits when

  • The failing section is one branch or one bathroom.
  • Other pipes look clean when we cut into them.
  • The leak was caused by a fitting, not pipe wall.
  • You plan to remodel one area soon.

Full repipe fits when

  • Galvanized pipe runs through the whole house.
  • Leaks keep showing up in different rooms.
  • Pressure is poor at every fixture.
  • Water runs rust-colored from several taps.
How it works

How a repipe actually runs

It is invasive, so the plan matters more than the pipe.

1

Map and test

We trace every supply run, test pressure at several fixtures and open a small access to see the pipe condition.

2

Plan the routes

New lines go through basements, crawl spaces and attics wherever possible, so fewer walls need opening.

3

Cut in and connect

We shut the main, drain the system and install the new pipe, fixture by fixture.

4

Pressure test

The new system is tested before any wall is closed, and we look for weeping at every joint.

5

Patch and restore

We close the access holes we made. Paint and finish carpentry are normally your call.

Reynoldsburg homes, hard water and who may do the work

Reynoldsburg was incorporated in 1839, and homes in town were built across many decades. Pipe material depends on the decade a house was built and any later work, so check what you have instead of assuming. A magnet will tell you if an exposed pipe is steel, which sticks, or copper, which does not.

Columbus softens water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon, and lists Reynoldsburg as a contract community. That is moderate hardness, but scale still builds in old steel lines and water heaters over decades.

Ohio licenses plumbing contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, and you can check a license through Ohio's online license lookup. For a job this large, also ask Franklin County Public Health whether an inspection applies to your address.

Cost factors

What a repipe quote depends on

A repipe is priced by house, not by foot, so these points drive the figure.

House size and layout

Bathrooms, kitchens and floors multiply the number of runs.

Access

An open basement ceiling is far easier than plaster walls with no crawl space.

Material choice

PEX, copper and CPVC differ in labor and parts.

Repair after

Patching drywall and plaster is separate from plumbing and varies widely.

Fixtures

Old shutoff valves and fixture connections often get replaced during the work.

Replacing old pipe is usually treated as maintenance, not a covered loss. Damage from a sudden burst may be covered, so ask your insurer how your policy reads.

Good to know

Before you sign for a whole-house repipe

Ask for the pipe material, the number of openings in walls, who patches drywall, and who pulls any required inspection. Two quotes can differ because one covers patching and one does not. Compare scope line by line, not total to total.

Checklist

Quick home checks before a repipe visit

These help us scope the visit.

  • Find the pipe where it enters near the water meter.
  • Test it with a magnet to learn if it is steel.
  • Run hot and cold at two fixtures together and listen.
  • Fill a clear glass first thing and look at the color.
  • Note any past leak repairs and their locations.
