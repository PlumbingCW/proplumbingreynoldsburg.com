Commercial plumbing means the same trade as a house, applied to bigger loads: more fixtures, grease, floor drains, backflow devices and longer pipe runs. The work below is what we actually do for Franklin County tenants and owners.

What to look for

Problems we are called to most

These are the faults that show up in shops, kitchens and small offices.

Slow floor drains

Water pools at the drain after mopping or dish-machine cycles. Usually grease, lint or a belly in the line.

Restroom stoppages

Paper towels flushed down a commercial toilet are a common cause. The fix is a cable first, a camera if it repeats.

Grease line backups

Kitchen sinks drain slowly, then smell, then back up. The interceptor is usually full or the line past it is coated.

Hidden supply leaks

A water bill climbs or a ceiling tile stains, but no fixture is dripping. The leak is inside a wall or under a slab.

Lift pump alarms

A basement restroom or mop sink that drains to a pump stops emptying. A stuck float or clogged impeller is the usual cause.

Commercial drain service and floor drain overflow

For a stoppage in a kitchen or restroom line, we start with a cable sized to the pipe. A cable punches a hole through the blockage but leaves the greasy wall coating behind, so a drain that clogs again in a few weeks usually needs a high-pressure water jet to scour the pipe wall. We then run a camera if the line has backed up more than once.

Floor drains that overflow with no obvious grease often tie to a collapsed section or a trap that dried out and let sewer gas back into the room. If the floor drain empties to a sump and lift pump, we check the float, the check valve and the discharge line before blaming the drain itself.

Commercial leak detection: what we can and cannot do

We can find a leak in pressurized water piping. That starts with a shutoff test at the meter to prove water is actually moving, then pressure testing of branches, then acoustic listening gear on exposed pipe and floors to pinpoint the spot before anything is opened. It saves a lot of drywall. For the home version of this work see our leak detection service.

We cannot promise to find every leak. Roof leaks belong to a roofer, and fire suppression lines are a separate specialty we do not service. Leaks under thick slabs or inside shared walls may need access that only the landlord can grant, and sometimes the honest answer is that the source is not plumbing at all.

How it works

How we run a grease trap cleaning

The work is simple on paper and messy in practice, which is why it gets skipped.

1

Open and inspect

We pull the lid, note the fat cap thickness and check baffles and the outlet tee for cracks.

2

Pump it out

Liquid, fat and settled solids all come out, not just the top layer. Skimming alone leaves the sludge that causes odors.

3

Scrape and rinse

Walls and baffles get scraped so the trap holds its full volume again.

4

Check the line

We look at the pipe leaving the trap. If it is coating up, jetting is the next step.

Act first

When a commercial drain or line failure is an emergency

Sewage on a floor where food is handled, a main line that will not drain, or a pipe running water into a ceiling over inventory are all reasons to call after hours. Our dispatch runs around the clock and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

  • Close the valve nearest the leak, or the main shutoff if unsure.
  • Stop using sinks and toilets on the affected drain line.
  • Keep staff and customers away from standing sewage water.
  • Cut power to anything sitting in water only if the breaker is dry to reach.
  • Take photos of damage for your insurer before you start cleanup.

Backflow testing and why it matters

A backflow assembly sits on a water line to stop dirty water from being pulled back into the clean supply. Springs and rubber seats wear, so the device gets tested with gauges to see whether the check valves still hold. We run that test and repair or replace worn parts when it fails.

Rules on which devices need testing, how often, and who receives the report come from your water provider. Ask your water utility before scheduling, so the test is done on the right form and the results reach the right office.

Storm drain and catch basin cleaning

Parking lots drain to catch basins with a sump below the outlet pipe. Silt, leaf litter and cigarette butts settle there until the basin is half full and the next downpour has nowhere to go. We vacuum or scoop the basin, then flush the outlet line to confirm water leaves freely.

If the pipe past the basin is cracked or crushed, flushing will not cure it and we will tell you so.

Cost factors

What shapes a commercial plumbing bill

Business jobs vary more than house jobs, so the final figure depends on these factors.

Access

A drain in an open back room is faster to reach than one behind a fixed wall or under equipment.

Pipe size and length

Larger lines need bigger equipment, and long runs take longer to clear or test.

Hours

After-hours work to avoid closing your doors is scheduled differently than daytime work.

Volume to haul

A trap or basin that has gone a long time between visits holds more waste to remove.

Business property or interruption coverage may respond to sudden water damage but often not to gradual leaks or neglected maintenance. Check your policy and your lease with your insurer or landlord before assuming either way.

Checklist

Quick checks before you phone a plumber

A few quick looks help us come prepared.

  • Find your main water shutoff and make sure it turns.
  • Note which fixtures are slow and which still drain.
  • Look for water stains on ceiling tiles below restrooms.
  • Read the water meter, wait an hour with everything off, read again.
  • Ask your landlord who owns the line in question.
